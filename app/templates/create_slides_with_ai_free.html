A Repeatable Slide Outline (Works for Most Decks)

If you’re not sure what slides to generate, start from a simple outline. This prevents the “random slide” effect and gives you a deck that feels intentionally paced. You can shorten or expand it depending on the time limit.

  1. Context: what’s happening and why it matters.
  2. Key message: the one takeaway you want remembered.
  3. Evidence: 2–3 slides with proof (metrics, examples, screenshots).
  4. Options: if there’s a decision, show trade-offs.
  5. Recommendation: what you suggest and why.
  6. Next steps: owner, timeline, and the decision required.

Make the Slides Specific (Small Details Improve Quality)

You don’t need a long prompt. You need a few concrete details that your audience cares about. Add two or three specifics and the generated slides become much more credible.

  • Numbers: targets, KPIs, revenue, cost, dates.
  • Names: product name, customer segment, team owners.
  • Constraints: time limit, slide count, tone (executive vs detailed).
  • Must-include slides: timeline, risks, KPIs, next steps.

Common Mistakes (and Quick Fixes)

If the slides feel repetitive, shorten the deck and make the titles more specific. If the deck feels “too safe,” add one slide that states the recommendation clearly. If the content is too dense, replace paragraphs with a single chart, screenshot, or example.
