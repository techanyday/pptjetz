How to Use an Unlimited Generator (The Right Way)

Unlimited generation is powerful when you iterate deliberately. Change one variable at a time so each version is an experiment.

  1. Define the room: who is the audience and what decision is needed?
  2. Choose a slide count: shorter is usually clearer.
  3. List must-have sections: KPIs, timeline, risks, next steps.
  4. Generate and review for gaps and repetition.
  5. Generate again with updated constraints to fix the biggest issue.

A Fast Polishing Checklist

The final deck quality depends on a short edit pass. This is the step that turns a draft into a professional presentation.

  • Rewrite titles as conclusions and ensure each slide has one message.
  • Add evidence to support each major claim.
  • Cut slide count and remove repetition.
  • End with next steps so the deck has an outcome.

Input Brief (The Smallest Amount of Context That Helps)

Unlimited generation works best when your input includes a few specifics. You don’t need a long prompt. You need the “room,” the decision, and a handful of facts.

Audience: [who will see it]

Decision needed: [approve, pick option, align on plan]

Slide count: [8–12]

Must include: [KPIs, timeline, risks, next steps]

Key facts: [3–6 metrics, dates, costs, examples]

If your source material is a long document, paste it as sections with headings. You’ll get cleaner slides when the text is easy to segment.
