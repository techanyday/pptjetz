What strong executive decks usually do

  1. 1. Put the conclusion early. Slide one should explain the state of play, not make leaders wait for the point.
  2. 2. Make metrics interpretable. Numbers are more useful when the slide says what changed and why it matters.
  3. 3. Surface risk honestly. A strong deck makes trade-offs and mitigation plans explicit.
  4. 4. End with a clear ask. Approval, escalation, resourcing, or alignment should be obvious.

What to include in the brief

Audience

CEO, exec team, steering committee, or board observers.

Top metrics

Current value, target, trend, and the headline explanation.

Risks + decision points

What could derail the plan and what leadership needs to decide now.

Next-step plan

Owners, timing, dependencies, and what changes after the meeting.

Helpful follow-ups: KPI dashboards, roadmap slides, and risk matrices.
